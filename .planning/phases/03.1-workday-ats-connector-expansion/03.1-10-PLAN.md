  <behavior>
    Closure isolation:
    - Two tenants processed together where tenant A's observation is a hard failure and tenant B's is complete and credible: tenant A's stored open jobs are all preserved with no closures and no lost last-success timestamp, and tenant B's genuinely absent jobs close normally.
    - The same with tenant A returning a schema-drift degradation, and again with tenant A returning a provider-confirmed zero: in all three cases tenant A closes nothing.

    Health isolation:
    - Tenant A's failure increments only tenant A's consecutive-failure count and writes only tenant A's error code; tenant B's health fields are untouched.
    - Tenant A's failure does not clear or advance tenant B's last-success timestamp.

    Scheduling isolation:
    - Tenant A being rejected by the dispatch gate for insufficient proof does not change tenant B's dispatch outcome when B has its own proof.
    - Tenant A's failure does not consume, block, or reset tenant B's activation progress: evidence acceptance is keyed on the company identifier, so a failing tenant cannot advance or stall another's window count.

    Promotion isolation:
    - Three accepted windows for tenant A leave tenant B's activation state and success counter unchanged.
  </behavior>

    Drive the closure and health cases through the pure lifecycle and ingest functions with two distinct tenant fixtures, asserting per tenant. Drive the scheduling cases through the dispatch gate from plan 07 with two company fixtures differing only in recorded proof.

    For the promotion case, assert against the migration text and the per-company keying rather than against a live database: the evidence ledger's unique constraint and its counting query are both scoped to the company identifier, so assert those scopes are present in the SQL and that no cross-company aggregation exists in either the claim predicate or the promotion branch.
