# Phase 03.4: Replace Automatic AI Scoring with Deterministic Ranking - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-07-23
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Remove every scheduled or background AI job-scoring path and replace the current AI-produced score, tiers, reasons, and gaps with an owner-configurable deterministic rubric. Preserve job ingestion, preferences, watchlist behavior, company controls, Dashboard structure, detail/apply/dismiss flows, authentication, RLS, resume management, and Phase 4 tailoring. AI resume extraction and future tailoring are not removed by this phase; only automatic job scoring is removed.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Deterministic ranking formula

- **D-01:** The default score is additive and totals 100 points.
- **D-02:** A strict target-title match earns 30 points. Strict means a normalized, case-insensitive target-title phrase appears in the job title.
- **D-03:** A weak target-title match earns 20 points. Weak means every significant target-title concept appears in the job title regardless of order, using the existing approved synonym and conservative inflection behavior.
- **D-04:** A preferred-location keyword match earns 10 points. The existing Locations preference becomes a soft keyword list for ranking; it is not a preferred-location hard filter.
- **D-05:** A job posted within the preceding 24 hours earns 10 points.
- **D-06:** A job from a company/source on the user's watchlist earns 10 points.
- **D-07:** An explicitly stated minimum required experience below the user's configured maximum earns 20 points. A requirement equal to or above the maximum earns zero points but does not hide the job. Missing, ambiguous, preferred, or non-required experience language earns zero points.
- **D-08:** Description include-keyword matches earn 3 points for one match, 5 for two, 10 for three, 15 for four, and 20 for five or more. Include keywords remain positive signals and never exclude a job.
- **D-09:** Preferences shows the complete scoring rubric. Every point value is editable. Validation requires category maximums to total 100; keyword steps must be nondecreasing and cannot exceed the keyword category maximum.

### Hard-filter boundaries

- **D-10:** A job with neither a strict nor weak target-title match never appears on the Dashboard.
- **D-11:** Jobs explicitly located outside the United States never appear. Blank locations and `Remote` without a stated country remain eligible; explicitly US and US-remote jobs remain eligible.
- **D-12:** Existing title-exclusion keywords remain hard title-only filters. Existing general Exclude keywords remain hard literal title-and-description filters.
- **D-13:** Preferred-location keywords and the maximum-experience preference are scoring signals only, not hard filters.

### Tiers and AI-score retirement

- **D-14:** Default tiers remain Strong 75–100, Good 50–74, and Weak 0–49.
- **D-15:** Preferences allows users to edit the Good and Strong thresholds, validating `0 < Good < Strong <= 100`.
- **D-16:** Before the Dashboard switches to deterministic ranking, every current job must be recalculated with the deterministic rubric. Users must never see a mixture of AI and deterministic scores.
- **D-17:** Historical AI scores, reasons, and keyword-gap data may remain only as non-displayed migration evidence. The active UI and ranking path must not read or present them.
- **D-18:** The existing match-reason/gap presentation is replaced by a deterministic category breakdown showing points earned, points possible, and the evidence that earned each category.

### Recalculation and presentation

- **D-19:** Saving preferences or rubric changes starts deterministic recalculation while the previous complete Dashboard remains visible with an `Updating` notice. The new result set replaces the prior set atomically only after the whole snapshot is complete.
- **D-20:** Newly ingested jobs appear only after hard filtering and deterministic scoring complete; there is no temporary Unranked or synthetic Weak state.
- **D-21:** The Dashboard keeps its current score/tier surface and existing newest-first default, explicit score sorting, company controls, tier controls, table, detail navigation, and other unrelated behavior.
- **D-22:** The scoring explanation appears on job detail as a rubric table. The removed Dashboard Match reason column does not return.
- **D-23:** If recalculation fails, keep the previous complete result set, show a bounded error with Retry, and never publish a partial or mixed rubric version.

### Automatic AI removal and deferred manual AI

- **D-24:** No cron, scheduled worker, preference save, resume event, ingestion event, retry, or background process may make an AI job-scoring request or reserve paid job-scoring capacity.
- **D-25:** Automatic AI job scoring is deferred rather than permanently forbidden. After Phase 4 tailoring is complete, a user may explicitly request one AI score from an opened job detail and may explicitly request a second score after tailoring a resume. Each call must be a visible, separate user action; no background scoring returns.

### the agent's Discretion

- Exact storage shape for versioned deterministic snapshots and archived AI migration evidence.
- Exact US-location normalization data and parsing implementation, provided it obeys D-11.
- Exact accessible form controls and validation copy for the rubric editor.
- Batch size and server-side processing mechanics, provided updates are free of AI calls and publish atomically.

</decisions>

<code_context>
## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets

- `cheapFilter` and its title synonym helpers provide the starting point for title/exclusion/US eligibility decisions.
- `Preferences` already has chip controls for titles, locations, include keywords, and exclusions; the physical experience column also remains for rolling compatibility.
- `tierPresentation`, `filterDashboardRows`, and the existing tier dropdown can consume deterministic scores without changing the surrounding Dashboard interaction.
- Revision/CAS fields on `user_jobs` provide concurrency protection, but the publication model must evolve into complete versioned snapshots to satisfy atomic switching.

### Established Patterns

- RLS scopes preferences and `user_jobs` to the signed-in owner; service workers perform protected pipeline writes.
- Company identity is truthful and provider-derived; watchlist membership must use existing canonical company/source ownership rather than display-name guessing.
- Dashboard company and tier controls are session-only, while rubric/preferences persist per user.
- Job detail is the only place that fetches full descriptions and presents detailed match evidence.

### Integration Points

- Preference saves currently flag all open `user_jobs`; Phase 03.4 must replace AI-hash invalidation with deterministic snapshot recalculation.
- `score-tick` is invoked every minute and owns filtering plus paid scoring. Planning must remove its AI path without interrupting job seeding/filtering or other scheduler functions.
- `listFeed` currently depends on scoring status/freshness fields. It must switch only after deterministic backfill is complete.
- Existing AI score/reason/gap fields must be isolated from active queries before deterministic production acceptance.

</code_context>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- Default editable rubric: title 30/20, preferred location 10, recency 10, watchlist source 10, experience 20, and include-keyword ladder 3/5/10/15/20.
- Rubric editor lives under Preferences and exposes the calculation rather than hiding ranking logic.
- Deterministic detail evidence should state exactly why points were earned, such as the matched title phrase, location keyword, watchlist source, explicit years requirement, posting timestamp, and matched include keywords.
- The transition must solve the current mass-refilter failure without buying more AI calls.

</specifics>
